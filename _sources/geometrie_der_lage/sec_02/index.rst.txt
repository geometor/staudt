§ 2. Parallelism and Infinitely Distant Elements (arts. 16–22)
==============================================================

.. rubric:: Karl Georg Christian von Staudt — *Geometrie der Lage* (1847), § 2 (arts. 16–22)

**Art. 16.** All straight lines in space that are parallel to a given straight line :math:`g` share a common *direction* (*Richtung*). Two parallel lines lie in a common plane and meet nowhere in finite space; three lines parallel to one and the same line are mutually parallel and form an axial sheaf of parallels (*Parallelstrahlenbündel*).

**Art. 18.** Because all lines of a parallel sheaf behave toward planes and external points in strict analogy with all lines passing through an ordinary point—every external point :math:`P` determines with the sheaf a unique ray through :math:`P` belonging to the sheaf, and every non-parallel plane cuts every ray of the sheaf—we adjoin to each parallel sheaf a single :term:`infinitely distant point <unendlich-fernes-element>` (*unendlich ferner Punkt* or ideal point :math:`P_\infty`) through which all lines of that direction pass.

**Art. 20.** All infinitely distant points of the straight lines lying in one plane :math:`\alpha` constitute a single *infinitely distant straight line* (*unendlich ferne Gerade* :math:`g_\infty`) of :math:`\alpha`; parallel planes share the exact same infinitely distant line. And the totality of all infinitely distant points and lines of three-dimensional space constitutes a single *infinitely distant plane* (*unendlich ferne Ebene* :math:`\omega_\infty`), cut by every ordinary plane along its ideal line.

**Art. 22.** Through this completion of space, every exception arising from parallelism disappears at a stroke:

1. Two distinct points (proper or ideal) determine one and only one straight line.
2. Two distinct coplanar straight lines share one and only one point.
3. Two distinct planes share one and only one straight line.
4. A plane and a straight line not lying in it share one and only one point.

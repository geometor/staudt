Critical Notes: § 8. Pure Synthetic Projectivity & The Fundamental Theorem (arts. 85–98)
========================================================================================

.. rubric:: Scholarly Commentary — Karl Georg Christian von Staudt — *Geometrie der Lage* (1847), § 8 (arts. 85–98)

* **The Klein–Darboux–Pasch History of Art. 96**: In Art. 96, von Staudt concluded that fixing three points :math:`A, B, C` fixes *every* point of the line because repeated harmonic fourths generate an everywhere dense Möbius net. :person:`Felix Klein <klein>` (:cite:`1873 <klein:1873>`) observed two subtleties:

  1. Density of the rational harmonic scale requires an **order and continuity (Archimedean/Dedekind) axiom**—subsequently supplied by :person:`Moritz Pasch <pasch>` (:cite:`1882 <pasch:1882>`).
  2. Over the complex field :math:`\mathbb{C}`, complex conjugation :math:`z \mapsto \bar{z}` preserves all harmonic cross-ratios :math:`(-1 = \overline{-1})` and fixes :math:`0, 1, \infty` without being projective! Over :math:`\mathbb{R}`, however, :person:`Jean-Gaston Darboux <darboux>` (:cite:`1880 <darboux:1880>`) proved that every harmonic-preserving bijection of :math:`\mathbb{P}^1(\mathbb{R})` automatically preserves positive squares (hence order and continuity) and is therefore strictly projective—completely vindicating von Staudt over the real line! Over an arbitrary field :math:`\mathbb{F}`, a harmonic-preserving bijection is *semilinear* (projective iff :math:`\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{F}) = \{1\}`).

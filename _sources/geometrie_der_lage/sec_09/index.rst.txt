§ 9. Involutions in Uniform Fundamental Forms (arts. 103–115)
=============================================================

.. rubric:: Karl Georg Christian von Staudt — *Geometrie der Lage* (1847), § 9 (arts. 103–115)

**Art. 103.** When a non-identical projectivity of a uniform fundamental form onto itself interchanges even a **single** pair of distinct elements (:math:`A \mapsto A'` and :math:`A' \mapsto A`), it interchanges **every** element with its image (:math:`X \leftrightarrow X'`) and is called an **involution** (*Involution*).

*Proof.* By the Fundamental Theorem (Art. 96), a projectivity is uniquely determined by three pairs of corresponding elements. Consider the given projectivity :math:`\tau` sending :math:`(A, A', B) \mapsto (A', A, B')` together with its inverse :math:`\tau^{-1}`, which sends :math:`(A', A, B') \mapsto (A, A', B)`. On the projective carrier, the ordered tetrad :math:`(A, A', B, B')` is projectively equivalent to :math:`(A', A, B', B)` via the quadrangle interchange of conjugate pairs (§ 6, Art. 72)! Hence :math:`(A, A', B) \mapsto (A', A, B')` also carries :math:`B' \mapsto B`, so :math:`\tau = \tau^{-1}` everywhere. :math:`\blacksquare`

**Art. 106 ( Quadrangle Theorem of Desargues–Staudt ).** The three pairs of opposite sides of any :term:`complete plane quadrangle <vollstaendiges-viereck>` :math:`EFGH` are cut by every transversal :math:`\ell` not passing through a vertex in three pairs of an involution.

**Art. 109 (Hyperbolic and Elliptic Involutions).** Two distinct pairs :math:`(A, A')` and :math:`(B, B')` uniquely determine an involution:

1. If :math:`(A, A')` and :math:`(B, B')` **do not** :term:`separate one another <getrennte-paare>`, the involution is *hyperbolic* and possesses exactly **two real double elements** (*Ordnungselemente* :math:`M, N`), which are simultaneously harmonic conjugates with respect to **every** conjugate pair :math:`(X, X')` of the involution: :math:`(M, N; X, X') = -1`.
2. If :math:`(A, A')` and :math:`(B, B')` **do** separate one another, corresponding points traverse the closed carrier in the same cyclical sense and never meet; the involution is *elliptic* and possesses **no real double elements** *(in 1856 Beiträge Heft 1, von Staudt represents a pair of conjugate imaginary points synthetically as an elliptic involution equipped with a cyclical sense!)*.

§ 19. Curves of the Second Order & Polar Generation (arts. 242–257)
===================================================================

.. rubric:: Karl Georg Christian von Staudt — *Geometrie der Lage* (1847), § 19 (arts. 242–257)

**Art. 242 (§ 19 — Von Staudt's Intrinsic Definition of Conics).** In a planar polar system :math:`\pi`, either no point lies upon its own polar (*elliptic polar system*), or infinitely many points lie upon their own polars (*hyperbolic polar system*). In the latter case:

* The locus of all points :math:`X` of the plane that lie upon their own polars (:math:`X \in \pi(X)`) is called a **curve of the second order** or **order curve** (:term:`Ordnungscurve <ordnungscurve>` — a point-conic).
* Dually, the envelope of all straight lines :math:`x` of the plane that pass through their own poles (:math:`\pi(x) \in x`) is a **curve of the second class** (*Hüllcurve* — the tangent-conic of the exact same curve).

.. picture:: /_static/figures/unit3_polar_conic_self_conjugate.svg
   :alt: Planar polar system defining a conic section as the self-conjugate locus X in pi(X), with exterior pole P, polar p = [T1 T2], and harmonic secant (T_A, T_B; P, Q) = -1.
   :caption: **Von Staudt's Polar Definition of the Conic (§§ 18–19, arts. 235–249):** The conic :math:`\mathcal{C}` is defined purely as the locus of self-incident points :math:`X \in \pi(X)`. From an exterior pole :math:`P`, the polar :math:`p = [T_1 T_2]` joins the contact points of the self-conjugate tangents, and every secant through :math:`P` is cut harmonically: :math:`(T_A, T_B; P, Q) = -1`.

**Art. 245.** Every straight line :math:`g` in the plane meets an order curve :math:`\mathcal{C}` in **at most two points** (namely the double points, if real, of the involution of conjugate points induced by :math:`\pi` upon :math:`g`):

* If the induced involution on :math:`g` is elliptic, :math:`g` is an *exterior line* (*Sekantenlose*) meeting :math:`\mathcal{C}` in zero real points.
* If :math:`g` lies on its own pole :math:`T = \pi(g)`, the involution degenerates at :math:`T`: :math:`g` meets :math:`\mathcal{C}` in the **single contact point** :math:`T` and is the **tangent** to :math:`\mathcal{C}` at :math:`T`.
* If the induced involution on :math:`g` is hyperbolic with double points :math:`T_A, T_B \in \mathcal{C}`, :math:`g` is a **secant** cutting :math:`\mathcal{C}` in :math:`T_A` and :math:`T_B`, and every pair of conjugate points :math:`(P, Q)` on :math:`g` divides :math:`(T_A, T_B)` harmonically: :math:`(T_A, T_B; P, Q) = -1`.

**Art. 253 (Steiner's Generation & Pascal–Brianchon Duality as Corollaries).** Because the pencil of rays joining a variable point :math:`X \in \mathcal{C}` to two fixed points :math:`A, B \in \mathcal{C}` corresponds projectively to the range of intersections of the polar :math:`\pi(X)` (the tangent at :math:`X`) with the fixed tangents at :math:`A` and :math:`B`, :person:`Steiner <steiner>`'s projective pencil generation of conics, :person:`Pascal <pascal>`'s inscribed Hexagon Theorem, and :person:`Brianchon <brianchon>`'s circumscribed Hexagon Theorem follow immediately without a single cone in three-space or quadratic equation!

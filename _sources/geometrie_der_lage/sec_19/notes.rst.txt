Critical Notes: § 19. Curves of the Second Order & Polar Generation (arts. 242–257)
===================================================================================

.. rubric:: Scholarly Commentary — Karl Georg Christian von Staudt — *Geometrie der Lage* (1847), § 19 (arts. 242–257)

* **Why von Staudt's Conic Surpassed Apollonius and Steiner**: For two millennia since Apollonius, conics were defined metrically via three-dimensional circular cones or focal distances; in 1832 (:cite:`Systematische Entwickelung <steiner:1832>`), :person:`Jakob Steiner <steiner>` defined conics in the plane as intersections of projective ray-pencils, **breaking point-line duality** at the moment of definition (requiring separate theorems for point-conics and tangent-conics). By defining the conic in § 19 (Art. 242) via an involutory :term:`planar polar system <polarsystem>` :math:`\pi` as :math:`X \in \pi(X)` and :math:`x \ni \pi(x)` **simultaneously**, :person:`von Staudt <staudt>` made :term:`projective reciprocity <reciprocitaet>` built into the very definition of the curve!

* **Cross-Reference Across GEOMETOR**: Compare ``geometor/pappus`` (Pascal and Brianchon hexagon configurations) and ``geometor/altshiller-court`` (Chapters IX–X on involution and poles/polars of the circle).

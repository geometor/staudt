Unit 1 Critical Notes: From Parallel Sheaves to Pasch & Lemoine
===============================================================

.. rubric:: Scholarly & Constructive Commentary on *Geometrie der Lage* (1847), §§ 1–5

* **Straightedge-Only Complexity (``S = R``)**: By forbidding compass circles from the outset (*Vorwort*), :person:`von Staudt <staudt>` restricts every construction to pure straightedge incidences (`assert_straightedge_only(model)` in ``geometor.staudt.projective``). In Émile Lemoine's *Geometrography* (``geometor/lemoine``), every operation in ``geometor.staudt`` belongs exclusively to straightedge primitives $R_1$ (placing the ruler through a point) and $R_2$ (drawing the straight line), with compass count $C_1 = C_2 = C_3 = 0$.
* **From Euclid's Fifth Postulate to Ideal Sheaves**: In § 2 (arts. 16–22), von Staudt still presupposes Euclidean parallelism to define ideal points as common directions. Thirty-five years later, :person:`Moritz Pasch <pasch>` (:cite:`1882 §§ 8–9 <pasch:1882>`, see :ref:`Codex II Unit 2 <pasch-unit-2-en>`) purged even this Euclidean parallel postulate by defining an *ideal point* intrinsically as a spatial **sheaf of lines** (*Strahlenbündel*) generated by two coplanar lines or three planes—a construction valid equally in hyperbolic, elliptic, and Euclidean space!
* **Steiner's Straightedge Parallel Specialization**: When an affine carrier $\ell = [AB]$ is accompanied by a single parallel rail $m = [U_0 U_1] \parallel \ell$ representing the shared ideal point $P_\infty$, ``construct_parallel_straightedge`` in ``geometor.staudt.throws`` executes :person:`Jakob Steiner <steiner>`'s (:cite:`1833 <steiner:1833>`) two-stage trapezoid midpoint and harmonic cevian construction—including automatic dualization across the midline locus where $m$ bisects $R$ and $\ell$.
* **Cyclical Separation vs. Open Betweenness**: Von Staudt's insight in § 4 (art. 36) that a single point cannot divide a closed projective line, whereas two pairs $(A, B)$ and $(C, D)$ can :term:`mutually separate <getrennte-paare>`, became the direct starting point for :person:`Giovanni Vailati <vailati>` (:cite:`1895 <vailati:1895>`) and :person:`Bertrand Russell <russell>` (:cite:`1903 Ch. XLV <russell:1903>`).

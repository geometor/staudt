Unit 1 Summary: Pure Incidence, Ideal Completion & Duality
==========================================================

.. rubric:: Analytical Synopsis of *Geometrie der Lage* (1847), *Vorwort* & §§ 1–5

1. **Methodological Autonomy (*Vorwort*)**: :person:`Von Staudt <staudt>` breaks decisively with :person:`Poncelet <poncelet>`, :person:`Möbius <mobius>`, :person:`Steiner <steiner>`, and :person:`Chasles <chasles>` by refusing to prove projective theorems via metrical segment lengths or arithmetic cross-ratios.
2. **Spatial Incidence (§ 1)**: Straight lines and planes are characterized by incidence determination ($A, B \mapsto [AB]$; non-collinear $A, B, C \mapsto \text{plane } ABC$; two meeting planes share a whole line).
3. **Adjoining Ideal Elements via Parallel Sheaves (§ 2)**: Each parallel direction (*Richtung* / *Parallelstrahlenbündel*) defines an :term:`ideal point <unendlich-fernes-element>` $P_\infty$; coplanar directions form an ideal line $g_\infty$, and all directions in space form the single ideal plane $\omega_\infty$, eliminating all parallel exceptions.
4. **Three-Dimensional Reciprocity & Cyclical Order (§§ 3–4)**: Adjoining $\omega_\infty$ makes *point* and *plane* strictly dual in 3-space (and *point* and *line* dual in 2-space) while turning every straight line into a closed cycle where separation requires **four** points ($(A, B)$ separating $(C, D)$).
5. **Uniform Fundamental Forms (§ 5)**: Defines the three projectively equivalent 1D carriers—point-range (*gerades Gebilde*), flat ray-pencil (*ebenes Strahlbüschel*), and axial plane-pencil (*Ebenenbüschel*)—connected by central projection and planar section (*perspektivische Lage*).

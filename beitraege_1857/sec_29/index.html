§ 29. The Harmonic Scale & Projective Abscissae (arts. 403–404)¶

Karl Georg Christian von Staudt — Beiträge, Zweites Heft (1857), § 29 (arts. 403–404)

Art. 403. Repeated addition of the unit element \(P_1\) produces on \(\ell\) the harmonic scale \(P_1, P_2, P_3, \dots, P_n, \dots\), where each consecutive triad \((P_{n-1}, P_n, P_{n+1})\) forms with \(P_\infty\) a harmonic tetrad \((P_{n-1}, P_{n+1}; P_n, P_\infty) = -1\). Taking additive inverses yields \(P_{-1}, P_{-2}, \dots\), and straightedge division (\(P_p \cdot P_q^{-1}\)) assigns a unique point \(P_{p/q}\) on \(\ell\) to every positive and negative rational number \(p/q \in \mathbb{Q}\).

Art. 404 (Intrinsic Projective Coordinates Without Measure). By Dedekind–Cantor continuity along the ordered carrier \(\ell\), every real point \(X \in \ell \setminus \{P_\infty\}\) is uniquely determined by the cut it induces in the rational harmonic scale and thereby receives an intrinsic real projective coordinate or abscissa \(x\)—constructed from pure incidence and order alone, prior to any concept of Euclidean length! When \(P_\infty\) is subsequently chosen to be the ideal point at infinity of an affine line, von Staudt’s projective abscissa specializes directly to the ordinary Cartesian coordinate \(x = \frac{\overline{P_0 X}}{\overline{P_0 P_1}}\).

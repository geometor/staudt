§ 8. Pure Synthetic Projectivity & The Fundamental Theorem (arts. 85–98)
========================================================================

.. rubric:: Karl Georg Christian von Staudt — *Geometrie der Lage* (1847), § 8 (arts. 85–98)

**Art. 93 (§ 8 — Pure Synthetic Definition of Projectivity).** Two :term:`uniform fundamental forms <grundgebilde>` are called **projectively related** (:term:`projektivisch <projektivisch>`) when their elements are placed in one-to-one correspondence so that **to every four harmonic elements of the one correspond four harmonic elements of the other**.

**Art. 96 (Fundamental Theorem of Projective Geometry).** *A projectivity between two uniform fundamental forms is uniquely and completely determined as soon as three distinct elements* :math:`A, B, C` *of the first form are assigned to three arbitrary distinct elements* :math:`A', B', C'` *of the second form*.

For from :math:`A, B, C` the fourth harmonic :math:`D_1` is uniquely determined by straightedge alone, and from any triad among :math:`\{A, B, C, D_1\}` further harmonic points :math:`D_2, D_3, \dots` are generated in an everywhere dense :term:`harmonic net <harmonische-reihe>`, each of whose images :math:`D'_k` is uniquely forced by the corresponding quadrangle construction on :math:`A', B', C'`. Hence if a projectivity fixes three distinct elements of one and the same carrier, it fixes **every** element of that carrier identically.

§ 18. Planar Polar Systems (arts. 235–241)
==========================================

.. rubric:: Karl Georg Christian von Staudt — *Geometrie der Lage* (1847), § 18 (arts. 235–241)

**Art. 235 (§ 18 — Planar Polarity).** A reciprocal correspondence (*Reciprocität* or correlation) in a plane assigns to every point :math:`P` a unique straight line :math:`p = \pi(P)` and to every straight line :math:`g` a unique point :math:`G = \pi(g)` such that collinear points correspond to concurrent lines and harmonic tetrads of points correspond to harmonic pencils of four rays. When this correspondence is *involutory*—so that whenever :math:`Q` lies on the line :math:`\pi(P)`, :math:`P` likewise lies on the line :math:`\pi(Q)`—it is called a **planar polar system** (:term:`ebenes Polarsystem <polarsystem>`), :math:`P` is the **pole** (*Pol*) of :math:`p`, and :math:`p` is the **polar** (*Polare*) of :math:`P`.

**Art. 238.** Three points :math:`A, B, C`, each of which is the pole of the opposite side (:math:`a = [BC], b = [CA], c = [AB]`), form a **polar triangle** (*Polardreieck* or self-polar triangle). A planar polar system is uniquely determined by a polar triangle :math:`ABC` together with one additional pole–polar pair :math:`(P, p)` in general position.

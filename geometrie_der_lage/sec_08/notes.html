Critical Notes: § 8. Pure Synthetic Projectivity & The Fundamental Theorem (arts. 85–98)¶

Scholarly Commentary — Karl Georg Christian von Staudt — Geometrie der Lage (1847), § 8 (arts. 85–98)

  • The Klein–Darboux–Pasch History of Art. 96: In Art. 96, von Staudt concluded that fixing three points $A, B, C$ fixes every point of the line because repeated harmonic fourths generate an everywhere dense Möbius net. Felix Klein (1873) observed two subtleties:

    1. Density of the rational harmonic scale requires an order and continuity (Archimedean/Dedekind) axiom—subsequently supplied by Moritz Pasch (1882).

    2. Over the complex field $\mathbb{C}$, complex conjugation $z \mapsto \bar{z}$ preserves all harmonic cross-ratios $(-1 = \overline{-1})$ and fixes $0, 1, \infty$ without being projective! Over $\mathbb{R}$, however, Jean-Gaston Darboux (1880) proved that every harmonic-preserving bijection of $\mathbb{P}^1(\mathbb{R})$ automatically preserves positive squares (hence order and continuity) and is therefore strictly projective—completely vindicating von Staudt over the real line! Over an arbitrary field $\mathbb{F}$, a harmonic-preserving bijection is semilinear (projective iff $\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{F}) = \{1\}$).

§ 18. Planar Polar Systems (arts. 235–241)¶

Karl Georg Christian von Staudt — Geometrie der Lage (1847), § 18 (arts. 235–241)

Art. 235 (§ 18 — Planar Polarity). A reciprocal correspondence (Reciprocität or correlation) in a plane assigns to every point $P$ a unique straight line $p = \pi(P)$ and to every straight line $g$ a unique point $G = \pi(g)$ such that collinear points correspond to concurrent lines and harmonic tetrads of points correspond to harmonic pencils of four rays. When this correspondence is involutory—so that whenever $Q$ lies on the line $\pi(P)$, $P$ likewise lies on the line $\pi(Q)$—it is called a planar polar system (ebenes Polarsystem), $P$ is the pole (Pol) of $p$, and $p$ is the polar (Polare) of $P$.

Art. 238. Three points $A, B, C$, each of which is the pole of the opposite side ($a = [BC], b = [CA], c = [AB]$), form a polar triangle (Polardreieck or self-polar triangle). A planar polar system is uniquely determined by a polar triangle $ABC$ together with one additional pole–polar pair $(P, p)$ in general position.

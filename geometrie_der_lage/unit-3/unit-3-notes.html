Unit 3 Critical Notes: Self-Conjugate Loci & Imaginary Elements¶

Scholarly & Constructive Commentary on Geometrie der Lage (1847), §§ 9 & 18–19

  • Why von Staudt’s Conic Surpassed Apollonius and Steiner: For two millennia since Apollonius, conics were defined metrically via three-dimensional circular cones or focal distances; in 1832 (Systematische Entwickelung), Jakob Steiner defined conics in the plane as intersections of projective ray-pencils, breaking point-line duality at the moment of definition (requiring separate theorems for point-conics and tangent-conics). By defining the conic in § 19 (Art. 242) via an involutory planar polar system $\pi$ as $X \in \pi(X)$ and $x \ni \pi(x)$ simultaneously, von Staudt made projective reciprocity built into the very definition of the curve!

  • Bridge to *Beiträge* Heft 1 (1856) — Synthetic Imaginary Points: Notice how § 9 (Art. 109) and § 19 (Art. 245) prepare von Staudt’s next triumph (Beiträge, Heft 1, 1856): when an exterior line $g$ fails to meet $\mathcal{C}$ in real points, $\pi$ still induces a completely well-defined real elliptic involution on $g$! Equipping that elliptic involution with one of its two opposite cyclical senses $(A B A' B')$ vs. $(A B' A' B)$ gave geometry its first rigorous, purely real synthetic model of conjugate complex intersections without $\sqrt{-1}$.

  • Cross-Reference Across GEOMETOR: Compare geometor/pappus (Pascal and Brianchon hexagon configurations) and geometor/altshiller-court (Chapters IX–X on involution and poles/polars of the circle).

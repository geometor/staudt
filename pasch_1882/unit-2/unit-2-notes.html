Unit 2 Critical Notes: Closing Euclid’s Gaps & the Birth of Modern Axiomatics¶

Scholarly & Constructive Commentary on Vorlesungen (1882), §§ 2, 8–12

  • Closing Euclid’s ``I.16`` and ``I.21`` Order Gaps (`geometor.staudt.pasch`):

    • In Elements I.16 (Exterior Angle Theorem, geometor/euclid & geometor/elements G.16), Euclid bisects $AC$ at $E$, doubles $BE$ to $F$, and assumes from the diagram that $F$ lies inside exterior angle $\angle ACD$. demonstrate_euclid_i16_pasch_gap() applies Pasch’s IV. Grundsatz to $\triangle ABD$ and transversal $[CF]$: since $B\text{--}C\text{--}D$ on side $BD$ while $[CF]$ meets $[BF]$ at $F$ beyond $E$, ray $CF$ is forced to pierce crossbar $AD$ internally at $K$ ($A\text{--}K\text{--}D$).

    • In Elements I.21, given interior point $D$ of $\triangle ABC$, Euclid writes “let $BD$ be drawn through to $E$ [on $AC$]” without proof. demonstrate_euclid_i21_pasch_gap() draws $[CD]$ meeting $AB$ at $M$ ($A\text{--}M\text{--}B$, $C\text{--}D\text{--}M$) and applies Grundsatz IV to sub-triangle $\triangle AMC$: since $[BD]$ enters open side $CM$ at $D$ and meets $[AM]$ externally at $B$, it must exit open side $AC$ at $E$ ($A\text{--}E\text{--}C$).

  • From Pasch § 12 (1882) to Hilbert’s Tables, Chairs, and Beer Mugs (1899) & Hoenen’s Critique (1954):

    • Pasch’s famous paragraph on p. 98 of § 12 is the verbatim philosophical fountainhead of David Hilbert’s Grundlagen der Geometrie (1899) (“One must be able to say at all times—instead of points, straight lines, and planes—tables, chairs, and beer mugs”), Bertrand Russell’s relational logicism (1903 Ch. I & XLVI), and Alfred Tarski’s first-order inner/outer Pasch axiom (1959).

    • Conversely, in geometor/hoenen (De Noetica Geometriae, 1954, Cap. IV–V), Fr. Petrus Hoenen, S.J. engages Pasch directly: praising Pasch for uncovering the true relational content of I.16 and planar separation, while arguing that our initial grasp of Stammsätze arises not from brute Humean induction over physical bodies, but from intellectual abstraction (judicium per phantasma) in intelligible extension.
